# Irish FBR — Document Acquisition Guide

**Purpose:** For every document the DFA may require for a Foreign Birth Registration application, this guide explains what it is, when you need it, how to get it (with country-by-country sources), common pitfalls, and direct links.

**Last verified:** May 2026  
**Disclaimer:** Not legal advice. Always verify current requirements at [ireland.ie](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/citizenship/born-abroad/registering-a-foreign-birth/).

## 1. Civil Birth Certificate

### What it is
An official civil registration of birth issued by the government registry of the country where the person was born. Must be the **long-form / full version** that shows the names of both parents.

### Who needs to provide one
- **You** (the applicant) — always required
- **Your Irish citizen parent** — always required
- **Your Irish-born grandparent** — always required (Route A)

### Critical rules
- **Long-form only.** The certificate must show parental details (mother's and father's names). Short-form extracts — which show only the registered person's name and date of birth — are **not accepted**.
- An "original" for FBR purposes means an **officially certified copy from the issuing registry**, not a photocopy of a family document. Freshly ordered certified copies are perfectly acceptable and preferred.
- Don't send irreplaceable originals — order a new certified copy.
- For pre-1864 Irish births, see [Section 14 (Baptismal Certificate)](#14-baptismal-certificate-pre-1864-births-only).
- A US Consular Report of Birth Abroad (CBRA) is **not accepted** — you need the actual birth certificate from the country of birth.

---

### How to get it — by country/region

#### 🇮🇪 Republic of Ireland (births 1864 onwards)

Civil registration began in 1864. Non-Catholic marriages were registered from 1845 but births only from 1864.

- **Online search and order:** [irishgenealogy.ie](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/) — free to search indexes; allows online certificate orders
- **GRO direct order:** [groireland.ie](https://www.groireland.ie/) — General Register Office, Werburgh Street, Dublin 2
- **HSE local offices:** [HSE Civil Registration Service](https://www2.hse.ie/services/births-deaths-and-marriages/get-certificates/birth-certificates/) — can order in person or by post from local registrar
- Cost: approximately €20 for a standard certified copy
- Turnaround: typically 5–10 working days by post; can be faster in person

> 💡 **Tip:** When ordering, explicitly request the **full/long form** birth certificate. In practice ROI certificates are almost always long-form by default, but it doesn't hurt to specify.

---

#### 🇬🇧 Northern Ireland (births 1864–present)

Northern Ireland has been a separate jurisdiction for registration purposes since 1922 — but civil records from 1864 onwards are accessible from **two different offices** depending on the period:

- **Births 1864–1921 (before partition):** Accessible from both the ROI GRO and GRONI
- **Births 1922 onwards:** [GRONI — General Register Office Northern Ireland](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/general-register-office)
  - Order online, by phone (0300 200 7890), or by post
  - Address: GRONI, Colby House, Stranmillis Court, Belfast BT9 5RR
  - Cost: approximately £15 for a standard certificate
  - Turnaround: under 2 weeks typically; same-day in person at Belfast office for higher fee

> 💡 **Tip:** If you're not sure whether your ancestor was born pre- or post-1922, try both GRONI and irishgenealogy.ie. Community members report GRONI is fast — one US applicant received their NI certificate in under 2 weeks.

---

#### 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scotland

- **National Records of Scotland:** [mygov.scot — Order a birth certificate](https://www.mygov.scot/order-a-birth-certificate)
- Also available from local registrars
- Cost: approximately £15

---

#### 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 England & Wales

- **UK General Register Office (GRO):** [gro.gov.uk](https://www.gro.gov.uk/gro/content/certificates/login.asp) — online ordering
- **Local register offices:** [gov.uk/register-offices](https://www.gov.uk/register-offices) — find your local office
- Cost: £12.50 online (GRO) or varies locally
- Turnaround: standard ~4 weeks online via GRO; faster in person at local office

> ⚠️ **Important:** Certificates ordered from the GRO online are the standard long-form version. However, some older records only have abbreviated entries — if you receive a short-form certificate, contact the GRO and request the full entry.

---

#### 🇺🇸 United States

US birth certificates are issued by **state** (not federal) vital records offices, and quality/format varies significantly by state.

- **Try your local city/county clerk first** — often faster and cheaper than state level
- **State vital records offices** — search "[state name] vital records birth certificate"
- **[VitalChek](https://www.vitalchek.com/)** — a national aggregator, officially recommended by the Irish Consulate General in San Francisco, but community experience is mixed:
  > ⚠️ VitalChek may not let you specify certificate format before ordering. Some applicants received short-form certificates and had to re-order. **Always specify "long form with parental details and raised seal"** wherever possible.
- Cost: $10–$30 depending on state
- Turnaround: varies widely — some states (e.g. Colorado) are fast (under 2 weeks); others (e.g. New York) can take 6+ months for additional copies

> ⚠️ **New York warning (community-reported):** NY State requires the person named in the record (or certain family members) to request it, and processing times for additional copies have exceeded 6 months. Order well in advance. Colorado, by contrast, processes quickly.

> 💡 **If born on a US military base abroad:** The birth certificate is issued by the country where the base is located, not the US. You need the certificate from that country's registry — not a CBRA.

---

#### 🇦🇺 Australia

- Each state/territory has its own registry. Key links:
  - NSW: [nsw.gov.au — births](https://www.nsw.gov.au/topics/births-deaths-marriages-and-civil-partnerships/births)
  - Victoria: [bdm.vic.gov.au](https://www.bdm.vic.gov.au/births)
  - Queensland: [qld.gov.au — births](https://www.qld.gov.au/births-deaths-marriages/births/get-a-birth-certificate)
  - All states: search "[state] Births Deaths and Marriages"
- Cost: approximately AUD $55–$65
- Turnaround: typically 10–15 business days

---

#### 🇨🇦 Canada

- Birth registration and certificate issuance is provincial/territorial
- Each province has a Vital Statistics or ServiceBC/ServiceOntario equivalent — search "[province] vital statistics birth certificate"
- Cost: approximately CAD $25–$45
- Turnaround: typically 2–6 weeks

---

#### 🌍 Other countries

For any other country, you need the **civil birth certificate issued by the national or regional civil registration authority** of that country. The Irish DFA does not specify a list of accepted issuing countries — any official government registry is acceptable. If the document is not in English or Irish, a [certified translation](#13-certified-translation) is required.

## 2. Civil Marriage Certificate

### What it is
An official civil registration of marriage, showing both spouses' names, dates, and typically their parents' names and places of residence.

### Who needs to provide one
- **You** — if you are or have been married (required to link your name to your birth certificate if your name has changed, and as corroborating lineage evidence)
- **Your Irish citizen parent** — if they were/are married, **regardless of whether their name changed**
- **Your Irish-born grandparent** — if they were/are married, **regardless of whether their name changed**

### Critical rules
> ⚠️ **The most common reason FBR applications are delayed.** The DFA website says "if applicable" but this has misled thousands of applicants. The community is emphatic: **if the person was married, the certificate is required — always.** It is not optional and not limited to name-change situations. Marriage certificates contain corroborating information (ages, birthplaces, parents' names) that the DFA uses to verify lineage. Omitting a marriage certificate typically results in a request arriving ~9 months in, placing you in a slower "additional documents" queue.

- Multiple marriages: include certificates for **all marriages** in the chain where relevant
- Maiden name certificates bridge surname changes across the chain — essential for proving e.g. that your grandmother (née Murphy) is the same person as the woman listed as your parent's mother on their birth certificate

---

### How to get it — by country/region

#### 🇮🇪 Republic of Ireland (marriages 1845 onwards)

Non-Catholic marriages from 1845; all marriages from 1864.

- **[irishgenealogy.ie](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/)** — search and order online
- **[HSE Civil Registration Service](https://www2.hse.ie/services/births-deaths-and-marriages/get-certificates/marriage-certificates/)** — order from local registrar or online
- **[groireland.ie](https://www.groireland.ie/)** — GRO direct
- Cost: approximately €20

---

#### 🇬🇧 Northern Ireland

- **[GRONI — nidirect.gov.uk](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/general-register-office)** — marriages registered since 1845 (non-Catholic) and 1864 (all)
- Cost: approximately £15

---

#### 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scotland

- **[mygov.scot — Order a marriage certificate](https://www.mygov.scot/order-a-marriage-certificate)**

---

#### 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 England & Wales

- **[UK GRO](https://www.gro.gov.uk/gro/content/certificates/login.asp)** — online ordering
- **[Local register offices](https://www.gov.uk/register-offices)**
- Cost: £12.50 online via GRO

---

#### 🇺🇸 United States

US marriages are registered at **county** level (not state or federal).

- Order from the **county clerk's office** where the marriage took place — search "[county name] [state] county clerk marriage certificate"
- Some states consolidate through state vital records — search "[state name] vital records marriage certificate"
- **[VitalChek](https://www.vitalchek.com/)** — available for some states
- Cost: $10–$30 depending on county/state
- Turnaround: varies; county clerks are usually faster than state offices

> 💡 **Tip:** If you don't know which county the marriage took place in, start with family knowledge (or genealogy sites like [Ancestry.com](https://www.ancestry.com/) or [FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/) to find the record, then order the official certified copy from the correct county).

---

#### 🇦🇺 Australia / 🇨🇦 Canada / 🌍 Other

Same approach as birth certificates — contact the relevant state/provincial Births, Deaths and Marriages office for the jurisdiction where the marriage was registered.

## 3. Change of Name Document (non-marriage)

### What it is
An official document recording a legal name change by means other than marriage — e.g. deed poll, statutory declaration of name change, or a court order.

### When needed
- **You** — if your current name differs from your birth certificate and you are not married (or if a prior name change occurred before a marriage)
- **Parent or grandparent** — same logic; if there is any break in the name chain across their documents

### How to get it

#### 🇮🇪 Republic of Ireland
- Deed poll enrolled in the High Court: contact the [Central Office of the High Court](https://www.courts.ie/offices-and-contacts) — Four Courts, Dublin 7
- Name change by statutory declaration: solicitor or notary public

#### 🇬🇧 United Kingdom (England, Wales, Scotland, NI)
- **Enrolled deed poll:** [UK Government — change your name by deed poll](https://www.gov.uk/change-name-deed-poll/enrol-a-deed-poll-with-the-courts)
- **Unenrolled deed poll:** prepared by a solicitor or via a reputable deed poll service; unenrolled versions are generally accepted
- **[GRONI name change recording](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/changing-your-name)** — Northern Ireland specific; form GRO231 (over 18) or GRO230 (under 18)

#### 🇺🇸 United States
- Court-ordered name change: obtain a certified copy of the court order from the issuing court
- Many states allow name change by marriage only — for other changes, a petition to the local court is required

## 4. Civil Death Certificate

### What it is
An official civil registration of death, showing the deceased's name, date, and place of death.

### When needed
A death certificate **replaces the photo ID requirement** for any person in the chain who is deceased. It is not needed if the person is alive and you can obtain a certified copy of their current ID.

- **Irish citizen parent (deceased):** death certificate replaces their photo ID
- **Irish-born grandparent (deceased):** death certificate replaces their photo ID

> 💡 **Tip:** Even if a relative is elderly, if they are still living, their photo ID is required — not their death certificate. Expired ID may not be accepted, so ensure living relatives' ID is current.

---

### How to get it — by country/region

#### 🇮🇪 Republic of Ireland (deaths 1864 onwards)
- **[irishgenealogy.ie](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/)** — free search; order online
- **[HSE Civil Registration Service](https://www2.hse.ie/services/births-deaths-and-marriages/get-certificates/death-certificates/)**
- **[groireland.ie](https://www.groireland.ie/)**
- Cost: approximately €20

#### 🇬🇧 Northern Ireland
- **[GRONI — nidirect.gov.uk](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/general-register-office)**
- Deaths from 1864 onwards
- Cost: approximately £15

#### 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scotland
- **[mygov.scot — Order a death certificate](https://www.mygov.scot/order-a-death-certificate)**

#### 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 England & Wales
- **[UK GRO](https://www.gro.gov.uk/gro/content/certificates/login.asp)**
- **[Local register offices](https://www.gov.uk/register-offices)**
- Cost: £12.50 online via GRO

#### 🇺🇸 United States
- Issued at **state** level (unlike marriage certs which are county-level)
- Search "[state name] vital records death certificate"
- **[VitalChek](https://www.vitalchek.com/)** works for most states
- Cost: $10–$25 depending on state

#### 🇦🇺 Australia / 🇨🇦 Canada / 🌍 Other
- Same as birth/marriage — contact the relevant Births, Deaths and Marriages authority for the jurisdiction of death

## 5. Photo Identification (certified copy)

### What it is
A photocopy of a current, valid government-issued photo ID document, certified by an appropriate professional as a true copy of the original.

### Who needs to provide one
- **You** — always (certified by your application form witness)
- **Your Irish citizen parent** — always, unless deceased (in which case death certificate is used instead; certified by a professional from the witness list)
- **Your Irish-born grandparent** — always, unless deceased (certified by a professional from the witness list)

### Accepted ID types
- Valid passport
- Valid national identity card
- Valid driving licence

> ⚠️ The ID must be **current and not expired**. One community member had their application returned because the ID copy they sent was of an expired document.

---

### How to certify a photocopy

The certifier must:
1. Make or obtain a photocopy of the ID
2. Write on it: *"Certified to be a true copy of the original seen by me"*
3. Sign and date it
4. Print their name underneath
5. Add their occupation, address, and phone number
6. Attach their official stamp/seal — **or** include a business card or their professional register reference number

The certifier for **your own ID** must be the same person who witnesses your application form (see [Section 8](#8-application-form-witnessed)).

The certifier for **your parent's or grandparent's ID** must be someone from the [witness profession list](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/citizenship/born-abroad/registering-a-foreign-birth/#Witnesses) — but does not need to be the same person as your witness.

---

### Where to get ID certified

#### In Ireland
- Any member of the professions on the [DFA witness list](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/citizenship/born-abroad/registering-a-foreign-birth/#Witnesses)

#### In the UK
- Solicitor, notary public, bank manager, pharmacist, teacher, GP, etc.
- Note: the same professions list applies regardless of country

#### In the USA
- **Notary public** is the most accessible option
- Available at banks (often free for account holders), libraries, UPS Store, FedEx/Kinkos, Staples
- **Known issue:** Some US state notaries cannot certify documents they have also signed. If a notary declines for this reason, try a different notary or a different profession (e.g. a licensed attorney or bank manager)
- Community tip: contact your local [Irish cultural centre](https://www.irishcentres.com/) for a referral

#### 🌍 Anywhere (for a relative's ID when you cannot be present)
- The relative themselves can have their ID certified locally by a relevant professional and post the certified copy to you
- You can then include it with your application

## 6. Proof of Address

### What it is
Two separate original documents confirming your current residential address. Both must be **originals** — photocopies are not accepted.

### Who needs to provide it
- **You** (the applicant) — always required: 2 separate originals
- **Parent (if making a minor's application)** — 2 separate originals for the parent

### Accepted document types
The DFA does not publish a definitive list, but community experience indicates the following are accepted:
- Utility bills (gas, electricity, water) — must be recent (within 3–6 months)
- Bank statements
- Official government letters (tax, social welfare, etc.)
- Property tax statements
- Vehicle registration documents
- Council/local authority correspondence

> 💡 **Tip:** Use documents from different issuers for your two proofs — e.g. one utility bill and one bank statement. Avoid using two documents from the same source.

---

## 7. Passport Photographs

### What they are
Standard passport-sized colour photographs of the applicant.

### Requirements
- **4 photographs** required in total
- **2 of the 4** must be signed and dated by your witness on the back, with a phrase such as *"I certify this is a true likeness of [name]"*
- **Do NOT attach photographs to the application form** — submit them loose
- If applying on behalf of a **minor**: 4 photos of the minor (2 witnessed) AND 4 photos of the parent/guardian (2 witnessed)

### How to get them
- Any photo booth (supermarket, post office, pharmacy)
- Professional photographer
- Self-printed if you have a suitable printer and background — but ensure they meet standard passport photo dimensions

### Standard passport photo specs
- 35mm × 45mm (standard international format)
- Plain white or off-white background
- Face centred, neutral expression, eyes open
- Taken within the last 6 months

## 8. Application Form (witnessed)

### What it is
The online FBR application form, completed electronically, printed, and signed in the presence of a qualified witness.

### How to complete it
1. Go to [fbr.dfa.ie](https://fbr.dfa.ie/)
2. Complete all sections online — **do not leave any section blank**
3. Pay the fee online (€278 for adults, €153 for under-18s)
4. Print the completed form
5. Sign the printed form **in the physical presence** of your witness
6. Your witness signs, stamps (or provides a business card), and certifies your ID copy

### The "greyed out grandparent tab" issue
> When the online form asks how your parent acquired Irish citizenship, it is asking about **your parent** — not you. If your parent was born abroad to an Irish-born grandparent, select **"Born abroad to a parent born in Ireland"**. The grandparent tab will then become available. Selecting the wrong option here is one of the most common mistakes in completing the form.

### Who can witness
The witness must:
- Be personally known to you (does not require a long-standing relationship — showing ID is sufficient)
- **Not be a family member**
- Be currently practising in one of these professions:

| Professions |
|---|
| Garda Síochána / Police Officer |
| School Principal / Vice Principal / Teacher / School Secretary / Pre-school Manager / Montessori Teacher / Lecturer |
| Member of Clergy |
| Medical Doctor |
| Nurse / Physiotherapist / Speech Therapist |
| Pharmacist / Dentist |
| Lawyer / Notary Public / Commissioner for Oaths / Peace Commissioner |
| Bank Manager / Assistant Bank Manager / Credit Union Manager or Assistant Manager |
| Accountant |
| Elected Public Representative |
| Veterinary Surgeon |
| Chartered Engineer |

> **If the witness has no official stamp:** provide their business card or a letter on headed paper instead.

### Finding a witness outside Ireland

#### UK
Any of the above professions — GPs, solicitors, teachers, pharmacists, and bank managers are the most commonly used.

#### USA
- **Notary public** is the most accessible option (banks, libraries, UPS Store, Staples)
- A licensed attorney (lawyer) also qualifies
- **Known issue:** some US state notaries decline to certify documents they've also signed. Try a different notary or profession if declined.
- Contact your local [Irish cultural centre](https://www.irishcentres.com/) if you are struggling

#### Everywhere
- Your parent, grandparent, and you can each use **different witnesses** — there is no requirement to use the same person for all documents

## 9. Foreign Birth Registration Certificate (parent's)

### What it is
The official A4 FBR certificate issued to your parent when they registered on the Foreign Births Register. It is their proof of Irish citizenship acquired by descent.

### When needed
Only in **Route B** — when your parent is an Irish citizen not by birth in Ireland, but because they themselves previously registered on the FBR.

> ⚠️ **Critical timing rule:** Your parent must have been registered on the FBR **before you were born** for you to be eligible. Check the issue date on their certificate. If they registered after your birth, you cannot claim through them.

### How to get it
- Your parent should already have their FBR certificate from their own registration
- If it has been **lost or stolen**, apply for a replacement at [fbrcertreplacements.dfa.ie](https://fbrcertreplacements.dfa.ie/)
  - Replacement requires: original birth certificate, certified photo ID, 2 proofs of address, 2 witnessed passport photos
  - The new certificate design includes a photograph of the holder
  - Address: FBR Replacement Certificate, Foreign Births Registration Section, PO Box 13003, Balbriggan, Co. Dublin, Ireland
  - There is currently a backlog for replacement certificates — allow extra time

## 10. Irish Naturalisation Certificate

### What it is
The certificate issued when an individual became an Irish citizen through the naturalisation process (i.e. by applying to the Minister for Justice after a period of legal residency in Ireland).

### When needed
Only in **Route B** — when your parent became Irish through naturalisation rather than by birth or FBR.

### How to get it
- Your parent should have the original from their naturalisation ceremony
- If lost, contact the **[Irish Naturalisation and Immigration Service (INIS)](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/)**
  - Phone: +353 1 616 7700
  - Application for replacement: write to INIS, Department of Justice, 13–14 Burgh Quay, Dublin 2, D02 XK70

---

## 11. Post-Nuptial Citizenship Certificate

### What it is
A certificate issued under older Irish law to foreign nationals who married Irish citizens and subsequently declared Irish citizenship. This route is no longer available for new applicants but certificates issued under it remain valid.

### When needed
Only in **Route B** — when your parent acquired Irish citizenship by post-nuptial declaration.

### How to get it
- Your parent should have the original certificate
- If lost, contact the **DFA Customer Service Hub**: +353 1 568 3331 or [webchat](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/citizenship/born-abroad/registering-a-foreign-birth/#Contact%20Foreign%20Birth%20Registration)

## 12. Adoption Certificate and Adoption Order

### What it is
The official legal documentation of an adoption — typically comprising the adoption certificate (registering the adoption) and the adoption order (the court order effecting the adoption).

### When needed
Two distinct scenarios:

**Scenario A — You were adopted:**  
If you were adopted by an Irish citizen, you may qualify if the adoption is recognised under Irish law. Contact the DFA directly before applying — this route has specific requirements.

**Scenario B — Your parent was adopted:**  
If your parent was born abroad and adopted by an Irish citizen (who themselves was born in Ireland), you need:
- Original adoption certificate and adoption order for your parent
- Original civil birth certificate of the Irish-born grandparent (adoptive parent)
- Original proof of Irish citizenship at the date of adoption coming into effect

### How to get it

#### 🇮🇪 Republic of Ireland
- **[Tusla (Child and Family Agency)](https://www.tusla.ie/services/alternative-care/adoption/tracing-and-information/)** — for adoptions registered in Ireland
- Adoption Authority of Ireland: [aai.gov.ie](https://aai.gov.ie/)

#### 🇬🇧 United Kingdom
- **England & Wales:** [UK GRO adoption certificates](https://www.gro.gov.uk/gro/content/adoptions/)
- **Scotland:** [National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/registration/registering-an-adoption)
- **Northern Ireland:** [GRONI](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/general-register-office) — adoptions from 1931

#### 🇺🇸 United States
- Contact the **court that issued the adoption order** — this is typically a county probate or family court
- Some states have centralised adoption records through the state vital records office

#### 🌍 Other countries
- Contact the **central adoption authority** in the country where the adoption took place — the DFA specifically requires the certificate to be issued by the central authority of that country

## 13. Certified Translation

### What it is
A translation of a document from a non-English/non-Irish language, produced and certified by a professional translator.

### When needed
Any document not in English or Irish must be accompanied by a certified translation. This applies to birth, marriage, and death certificates, as well as any other supporting document.

### Requirements
The translation must be:
- Produced by a qualified/professional translator
- Accompanied by a statement certifying the accuracy of the translation
- Signed by the translator with their contact details and qualifications

### How to get one

#### Finding a certified translator
- **[Association of Translation Companies (ATC)](https://www.atc.org.uk/)** — UK-based directory
- **[American Translators Association (ATA)](https://www.atanet.org/directory/)** — US-based directory with member search
- **[ITI — Institute of Translation and Interpreting](https://www.iti.org.uk/discover/find-a-language-professional.html)** — UK directory
- Solicitors and notaries sometimes provide translation services or referrals
- Your local consulate or embassy may maintain a list of recommended translators for their national language

#### Cost
Typically €50–€150 per document depending on language pair, length, and complexity. Rare or regional languages cost more.

> 💡 **Tip:** Some registries (e.g. in EU countries) will provide a certified translation as part of the certificate order process. Ask when ordering whether a bilingual or translated version is available.

## 14. Baptismal Certificate (pre-1864 births only)

### What it is
A church record of baptism, typically used when a civil birth certificate does not exist because the birth predates civil registration.

### When needed
**Only** when your Irish-born grandparent (or further ancestor) was born **before 1864** — the year civil registration of births began in Ireland (non-Catholic marriages from 1845).

> ⚠️ Baptismal certificates are **not** accepted as a substitute for civil birth certificates for births after 1864. Do not use them for post-1864 births.

### What it typically contains
- Child's name
- Date of baptism (and sometimes birth date)
- Parents' names
- Officiating minister's name
- Parish

### How to get one

#### 🇮🇪 Republic of Ireland
- **[IrishGenealogy.ie](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/)** — digitised Catholic and Church of Ireland parish records, searchable free
- **[National Library of Ireland — Catholic Parish Registers](https://registers.nli.ie/)** — digitised pre-civil-registration Catholic baptism records
- **[PRONI — Public Record Office of Northern Ireland](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/proni)** — NI church records including Church of Ireland, Presbyterian, Methodist
- **[RootsIreland.ie](https://www.rootsireland.ie/)** — transcribed Catholic records from many counties (subscription)
- Contact the relevant **local parish** directly — many hold their own registers or can direct you to archive copies

#### Finding the right parish
If you know the townland or area your ancestor was from, use **[IreAtlas Townland Database](https://www.seanruad.com/)** to identify the civil and Catholic parish, then search the relevant archive.

> 💡 **Important:** The DFA has confirmed it will accept a baptismal certificate for pre-1864 births. If submitting one, include a cover note explaining the birth predates civil registration (1864) and therefore no civil birth certificate exists.

## 15. School / Doctor Letter (minor applicants)

### What it is
A letter on official headed paper from a school, GP, or other relevant source that confirms the minor's address.

### When needed
Required for **all minor (under 18) applicants**, in addition to all other documents.

### What it must contain
- The minor's full name
- The minor's address
- Be on official headed paper
- Be signed by the issuing authority (school principal, GP, etc.)

### How to get it
- **From a school:** contact the school office/principal for a letter confirming the child is enrolled and their address
- **From a GP/doctor:** a brief letter from the family doctor confirming the child is registered at the practice and their address
- There is no standard form — a simple covering letter on headed paper is sufficient

---

## 16. Proof of Guardianship (minor applicants, non-parent applicant)

### What it is
Legal documentation demonstrating that the person making the FBR application on behalf of a minor has legal guardianship or parental responsibility for that child.

### When needed
Only when the person making the application is **not the child's parent** (i.e. a legal guardian, foster parent, or other person with parental responsibility).

### Accepted documents
- Court order granting guardianship
- Legal guardianship certificate
- Adoption order (if applicable)
- Any legal document establishing parental responsibility

### How to get it
- If you obtained a court order for guardianship, certified copies can usually be obtained from the issuing court
- Contact a solicitor if you are uncertain what documentation is appropriate for your jurisdiction

## 17. Affidavit / Statutory Declaration (estrangement or missing documents)

### What it is
A signed, witnessed written statement explaining why a normally required document cannot be obtained — most commonly used when an applicant is estranged from a living parent or grandparent whose photo ID is required.

### When needed
- You are estranged from a living parent or grandparent and cannot obtain a copy of their photo ID
- A document has been permanently lost or destroyed and cannot be re-ordered
- Any other exceptional circumstance making a required document genuinely impossible to obtain

### What it must contain
- A clear explanation of the situation (the estrangement, the reason contact is not possible, what steps you have taken to try to obtain the document)
- A statement that the information given is true
- Your signature
- Witnessed/sworn before a notary, commissioner for oaths, or solicitor (depending on jurisdiction)

### How it works in practice
Community experience confirms the affidavit route is effective. One applicant who was estranged from their father:
- Included a signed affidavit in the **original application** explaining the estrangement
- When the DFA emailed 9 months in requesting the father's ID, they replied with the same explanation
- Was approved approximately one week later — without ever providing the father's ID

> 💡 **Tip:** Write the affidavit clearly, factually, and without embellishment. Include: how long you have been estranged, why contact is not possible, and what you have done to try to resolve the situation. Include it in your original application proactively — don't wait for the DFA to ask.

### How to get it sworn/witnessed

#### 🇮🇪 Republic of Ireland
- Commissioner for Oaths or solicitor — [Law Society of Ireland solicitor search](https://www.lawsociety.ie/Find-a-Solicitor/)

#### 🇬🇧 United Kingdom
- Solicitor or notary public
- [Find a solicitor — Law Society England & Wales](https://solicitors.lawsociety.org.uk/)
- [Find a Scottish solicitor — Law Society of Scotland](https://www.lawscot.org.uk/find-a-solicitor/)

#### 🇺🇸 United States
- Notary public — widely available at banks, UPS Store, libraries, Staples

#### 🌍 Other countries
- Any notary public, commissioner for oaths, or equivalent local legal officer
